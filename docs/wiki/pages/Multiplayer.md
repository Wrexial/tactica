Multiplayer (マルチプレイ, Maruchipurei?), also called Multiplay Mode, allows more than one player to play a Final Fantasy game at a time. Some games allow two players to control the main party in a game, others allow the players to face off, while still others are MMORPGs and allow many players to coexist in the game simultaneously.

Multiplayer modes are generally divided into two categories: Player versus Environment (PvE) and Player versus Player (PvP). PvE modes, also called cooperative (co-op), see players work together against computer-controlled enemies, while PvP sees players battle against eachother in competitive gameplay.

## Appearances

### *Final Fantasy III*

A messaging mode called Mognet appears in the Nintendo DS version, available over local Wi-Fi network. Before the shutdown of the Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection service for Nintendo DS on May 20 2014, people had the possibility to trade friend codes and send letters via the Wi-Fi network. Unfortunately, this means that current DS players can no longer utilize the Mognet service to its fullest, and new players will only be able to use local DS-to-DS wireless communication to access the extra features it unlocks.

### *Final Fantasy IV*

Two players can play the SNES version. There is no visible option in the North American "Final Fantasy II" version to allocate the characters, but as long as a second controller is plugged in, a second player can give commands to any characters during battle. However, in the original Japanese version, there is an option in the config menu that allows each character to be assigned per controller, as is the case in Final Fantasy V and Final Fantasy VI.

A two-player Whyt battle mode appears in the DS remake.

#### *Final Fantasy IV: The After Years*

In the WiiWare version, players can assign party slots between four different controllers.

### *Final Fantasy V*

In the SNES and PS versions, players can allocate different party slots to the two different players, though player one still controls the party on the map.

### *Final Fantasy VI*

Similarly to Final Fantasy V, players can assign battle party members to player one or player two in the SNES and PS versions.

If one player presses any buttons while the other is attempting to input a Blitz command, the Blitz will fail.

### *Dirge of Cerberus -Final Fantasy VII-*

The Japanese version featured a multiplayer mode which serves as a prequel to the main game. It was not made available to overseas versions.

### *Final Fantasy VII Ever Crisis*

Co-op multiplayer was available across several modes of play, requiring parties of three for every co-op stage available. Individual players could preset up to 20 loadouts for co-op play, each with one unit fully configured. Co-op could start either with a randomized room and party, or be set up as a private room for players to request friends or guildmates to join in. Players could also use the built-in multiplier function to increase their rewards yields independently of their teammates. Players generally had up to three minutes per attempt to lock-in.

In practice, however, with multiple difficulty levels of a given co-op available, many players would often forgo lower tiers to achieve better rewards output, leaving valuable rewards such as Blue Crystals unclaimed. For this reason, it was considered best practice to play the lower-difficulty levels of a co-op track as soon as they become available.

If a probe for random matches took too long, an error message would be displayed offering the player an option to search again. If any player experienced connection issues, the room would close and the players would be returned to the title screen.

### *Final Fantasy IX*

As a throwback to Final Fantasy V and Final Fantasy VI, players can once again divide the party up between two players.

### *Final Fantasy X*

Final Fantasy X was originally going to include online elements via PlayOnline, but this feature was scrapped.

### *Final Fantasy XI*

Final Fantasy XI was the first massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG) in the series.

### *Final Fantasy XIV*

See also: PvP (Final Fantasy XIV)

Final Fantasy XIV followed Final Fantasy XI as an a MMORPG and features similar gameplay elements, though many of these were reduced or removed over time.

### *Final Fantasy XV*

The Comrades expansion adds cooperative multiplayer to the game, allowing players to create their own avatar and take on quests with up to three other players online.

Online functionality was removed from Comrades for Origin in June 2020.

#### *King's Knight -Wrath of the Dark Dragon-*

Co-op multiplayer was always available, but the comparatively small player base made its quests difficult to start, needing two or more players and the leader (host) could not start until all other players reported ready. The Final Fantasy XV collab partially resolved this by adding the four main cast members as AI players during designated timeframes. These quests would be best experienced with all four players active, as they could unleash a United Might attack against the boss of the quest.

There were four regular quests that cycled every 2–3 days until the start of the sunset period in late March 2018, each offering different upgrade materials per quest. Beyond this, event co-op quests would offer special exchange currency, which would be doubled in primetime hours.

### *Final Fantasy Type-0*

The PlayStation Portable version of Final Fantasy Type-0 includes multiplayer elements and has ad-hoc and online infrastructure features. The multiplayer allows players to call friends and people from other parts of the world to help them in battle and will run on a time limit. This allows SPP to be earned.

Multiplayer is not featured in the HD version, being replaced by the Support Personnel system as a means of obtaining SPP.

### *Final Fantasy Tactics*

The War of the Lions remake includes two multiplayer modes: Melee, where the two players can battle, and Rendezvous, where the two players team up and tackle a mission.

### *Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles*

To play in multiplayer mode, each player must use a Game Boy Advance as a controller and connected to the GameCube. Then, each Game Boy Advance will act as the player's personal screen where they can control their character and access an individual menu without disrupting the game's flow.

#### *Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Ring of Fates*

See also: Final Fantasy Crystal Chronicles: Ring of Fates missions

Multiplay Mode can be chosen at the title screen, and allows the player to either create a party, join a party, or play solo. Players can play Story Mode in almost its entirety without the story bits (excluding some parts such as replaying battles with early bosses at the Crystal Temple) or play various Missions, accepted at the castle in Rebena Te Ra.

### *Dissidia Final Fantasy*

Multiplayer is ad-hoc and can only be played between two people who are next to each other.

#### *Dissidia 012 Final Fantasy*

Multiplayer is the same as its predecessor, but includes the new gameplay features introduced.

#### *Dissidia Final Fantasy Opera Omnia*

Co-op multiplayer was always available, albeit restricted to certain events and scenarios.

Where co-op was available, it was marked with a badge indicating that the area had been optimized for co-op play. Entering a co-op area offered the player a choice to organize a group or go solo with the currently-active party. If the player went solo, they could pick a support unit to call for the battle. If the player entered group play, they had to select whether to become a host (party leader) or guest (party member).

As a host, the player could collect any target items offered in the battle, as well as having the ability to save high scores and collect points to pick up event items. Hosts could also call upon previous friends to form a party; or, they could use the Free Recruit mode to draw random players into a party. Each player had approximately five minutes to lock in; but with each player that joined, the timer reset. Once all players locked in, the host spent one of their five Link Bells to start the battle. Link Bells refilled at a rate of one per two real-time hours.

As a guest, no scoring or target items could be obtained, but the player could be picked up by a free-recruiting player and had the option of spending a Link Bell to collect extra spoils after battle.

Once battle had begun, each player controlled their unit in real-time. Each player could also call the active summon in their active party once during the battle. Units with an equipped BT Ability could also use it within the parameters defined by its ability level(s).

#### *Dissidia Duellum Final Fantasy*

Being a unique "PvPvE" game, the combat system is entirely built around a multiplayer experience. No other modes are possible. When players choose to battle, they are matched in real time with players of comparable rank and combat power. All players then have about five minutes to battle on a random street-corner field and race to eliminate the field boss. The first team to fell the boss wins the bout and claims all points and rewards therein, distributing said rewards to all team members and awarding higher-grade ability crystals as drops. The defeated team will have points deducted from their rank for the current season, but will also receive fewer progression points and a lower-ranked crystal drop.

### *War of the Visions: Final Fantasy Brave Exvius*

Certain events in the Farplane menu panel had accompanying Multi Quests accessed via a second, separate sub-menu. Once there, the player could choose any available mission to host or guest. As host, one could set party parameters and player entry conditions. Combat began when all necessary players had locked in and the host initiated.

### *Theatrhythm Final Fantasy*

The game features a multiplayer mode with four players called Chaos Shrine Mode. During multiplayer, all players receive the record of the group's best player, meaning a player can obtain valuable items by playing with good players.

#### *Theatrhythm Final Fantasy Curtain Call*

Versus Battle Mode is a new multiplayer mode, it can be selected from the main menu and played locally or online.

The aim is to get the highest score against the opponent in a Battle Music Sequence. The twist is that the players have an EX Gauge, which gradually fills during the song. When the gauge is filled the EX Burst activates and will cause negative effects to whoever is inflicted, thus changing strategies during battle.

### *Final Fantasy: The 4 Heroes of Light*

The game features a multiplayer mode in which up to four players can connect together via local wireless connection, and fight monsters together. Playing in multiplayer mode gets significantly more wireless points which can be spent in the wireless shop for weapons, armor, and accessories.
