> Description

Goblin in Final Fantasy Tactics Advance is the first race of enemies encountered. Goblins are blue, in contrast to their stronger counterparts, the Red Caps. While they make for an interesting battle, Goblins are usually easy to defeat and are considered the weakest enemy in the game.

Goblins are found in at least one mission in almost every one of the earlier locations, but cannot be encountered again after the early missions are completed (see below). Their attack, Goblin Punch, misses often, but when it does hit the target, it usually inflicts only a small amount of damage. Goblin Punch is learnable as Blue Magic, but due to its inconsistency and weakness, it is more of a trophy ability.

## Extinction

*Portrait.*

Fairly early on, Goblins will become "extinct", meaning they do not show up in any mission after the six listed above. Thus it is important to capture a Goblin and to teach all of the human members of the player's clan Goblin Punch as early as possible. This can be bypassed by simply avoiding to complete one of the missions, effectively letting players complete the storyline and still be able to battle Goblins afterwards. This can be a good idea, if one wishes to level up their characters, have them learn more abilities and possibly to have as many Hunters as possible, when trying to capture a Goblin.

## Etymology

The word "goblin" comes from the Norman French word Gobelinus, the name of a ghost that haunted the town of Évreux in the 12th century.

A goblin is a small, mischievous creature found in many European folk tales and legends. Their behavior and nature vary depending on the source. In European folklore they are usually depicted as trickster and vengeful creatures who pull pranks on humans, ranging from shapeshifting into animals to spreading illness. They are often associated with alcohol, pilfering liquor from breweries.

While most goblins are just mischievous, some legends depict them as malevolent, ferocious and murderous fiends who actively prey on humans, attacking them with stones and their makeshift pikestaves. They are known for lurking inside abandoned castles and will ambush travelers, killing and devouring their flesh, and then soak their knitted caps in the victim's blood. As truly demonic in nature, they can only be driven away by prayers or brandish of crucifixes.

However, in some cultures, goblins are depicted as benevolent household creatures that perform domestic chores and farming tasks for humans in exchange for food. They will often appear at night, while the humans are sleeping, as the food offering is left for them. However, if provoked or disrespected, these goblins will quickly abandon the household and pull pranks on their former masters.
